FDA-approved peptides: the real list

Approved, compoundable or research-only? Here's where every popular peptide actually stands, with a primary FDA source on every row.

The short version: nine of the peptides people ask about are real FDA-approved drugs. Retatrutide is close but still investigational. The trendy ones, BPC-157, TB-500, ipamorelin, CJC-1295, MOTS-c, Semax and Selank, are not approved, and compounders can't currently use them either.

What could change next

  • The July 2026 PCAC votes. FDA's compounding advisory committee voted to recommend BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, Semax and epitalon for the 503A list (FDA meeting page; vote report). The votes are advice. FDA hasn't added any of them yet.
  • An interim access policy. FDA sent "Interim Access Policy for Compounding Using Certain Peptides" (RIN 0910-ZD94) to White House review on Oct 14, 2026 (OIRA EO 12866 review entry, reginfo.gov). It hasn't appeared in the Unified Agenda or the Federal Register, isn't in effect, and which peptides it covers isn't public.
  • Injectable GHK-Cu. FDA says it plans to take GHK-Cu to the PCAC by the end of February 2027 (FDA 503A list).
  • Retatrutide. Lilly plans to submit to FDA in Q1 2027. Track it on our retatrutide FDA status page.

FDA status FAQ

Is BPC-157 FDA-approved?

No. BPC-157 is not an approved drug and is sold for research only. It's also not on FDA's list of bulk substances compounders can use while under review (FDA). A July 2026 advisory committee vote in its favor is a recommendation, not an approval.

Which GLP-1s are FDA-approved?

Semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy, Rybelsus), tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound), liraglutide (Victoza, Saxenda) and dulaglutide (Trulicity), per Drugs@FDA. Orforglipron (Foundayo) is approved too, but it's a small molecule, not a peptide. Retatrutide is still investigational.

Can a pharmacy compound peptides like BPC-157 or TB-500?

Not under current FDA policy. Neither is on FDA's category 1 list, and FDA lists both as nominated, then withdrawn, with safety concerns (FDA). The PCAC voted for them in July 2026, but FDA hasn't acted on that vote.

What is the FDA's interim peptide compounding policy?

A notice titled "Interim Access Policy for Compounding Using Certain Peptides" (RIN 0910-ZD94) reached White House review on Oct 14, 2026 (OIRA EO 12866 review entry, reginfo.gov). It hasn't appeared in the Unified Agenda or the Federal Register, isn't in effect, and its details aren't public yet.
