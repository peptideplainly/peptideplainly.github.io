Why is peptide info so confusing?

If you've watched three videos and heard three different answers, you're not alone, and it isn't your fault. Here's why it happens, and how to cut through it.

1. "Peptide" covers very different things

FDA-approved prescription drugs, skincare ingredients, food supplements, and Research purposes only compounds all get called "peptides." Advice that fits one doesn't fit another. Our guides label each one clearly.

2. Animal results get presented as human results

Many claims come from rat or mouse studies. These matter for science, but they often don't hold up in people. We always tell you which kind of study a claim comes from.

3. Dose numbers get copied without sources

For approved drugs, the official dose schedule is on the FDA label, and we link it. For research-only peptides there is no established human dose, so numbers online usually trace back to animal studies or personal anecdotes.

4. Money shapes the message

Some "educators" also sell products or earn affiliate commissions. That doesn't make them wrong, but it's worth knowing.

5. The science really is still moving

Regulations and evidence change, and in 2026 they changed for several peptides. We date-stamp our pages and link official sources.

How to get clear answers

  • Look for a "Quick answer" box and a source link on every claim.
  • Bring questions to a licensed clinician. Each peptide page has a list to take with you.
