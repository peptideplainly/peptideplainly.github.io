KPV: the 3-amino-acid peptide taking on gut inflammation

Research purposes only Checked against PubMed and official sources Oct 18, 2026

Why people are excited

The 3-amino-acid peptide for gut and skin calm.

  • Reduced colitis in two mouse models.
  • Blocked inflammation pathways in lab-grown human gut cells at nanomolar amounts (Gastroenterology, 2008).
  • Calmed inflamed skin cells in the lab.

Quick facts

  • What it is: a naturally occurring 3-amino-acid peptide (Lys-Pro-Val).
  • Status: research purposes only. Not FDA-approved.
  • Evidence: reduced colitis in mouse studies; calmed inflammation in human cells in the lab.
  • Human evidence: no published human trials.
  • Known risks: unknown human safety.

What is KPV?

KPV is a naturally occurring tripeptide (lysine-proline-valine), just three amino acids long, derived from the hormone alpha-MSH (Tissue Cell, 2025). It's getting buzz for gut health and inflammation, and it gets into gut cells through PepT1, a transporter that's turned up in inflamed colons (Gastroenterology, 2008).

Is KPV FDA-approved?

Not FDA-approved. In July 2026 an FDA advisory committee voted to recommend allowing it in pharmacy compounding (FDA meeting page; vote report). The vote is non-binding.

Not FDA-approved; sold for research only. Known risks: not well studied; there are no published human trials. Choose a tested source.

Dosage

There's no established human dose, so we don't list one.

What the research shows

  • Colitis, in mice (Dalmasso et al., Gastroenterology, 2008): KPV in drinking water reduced colitis in two mouse models, with lower inflammatory signals. In human gut and immune cells in the lab, tiny (nanomolar) amounts blocked key inflammation pathways.
  • Targeted delivery, in mice (Mol Ther, 2017): KPV packed into gut-targeting nanoparticles sped mucosal healing and cut inflammation in a mouse colitis model.
  • Skin cells (Tissue Cell, 2025): in lab-grown human skin cells (and a 3D skin model), KPV reduced cell death and inflammation caused by fine-dust air pollution.
  • We found no published human trials of KPV.

KPV FAQ

What is KPV used for?

Online, mostly gut health and inflammation. The research behind that is in mice and lab-grown cells, where it reduced colitis and inflammatory signaling.

Does KPV work in humans?

We found no published human trials.

Is KPV FDA-approved?

No. An FDA advisory committee voted in July 2026 to recommend allowing it in compounding, but that vote is non-binding.
