TB-500 (thymosin beta-4), explained plainly

What it is

Thymosin beta-4 (Tβ4) is a natural protein involved in cell movement and tissue repair. "TB-500" is the name used online for a synthetic product based on part of Tβ4. TB-500 itself is not the same molecule that has been tested in clinical trials.

Regulatory status

TB-500 is not approved for human use and is prohibited in sport by WADA. Thymosin beta-4 has been studied in early-stage clinical trials, for example as eye drops, but it isn't an approved medicine in the U.S.

Regulatory status as of October 2026. Rules vary by country and change over time.

What the human evidence says

  • Heart attack trial (Cardiovasc Res, 2025, PMID 41229390): recombinant human Tβ4 was tested in 96 heart-attack patients. The overall difference in infarct size vs. placebo was not significant. A benefit was seen only in a subgroup dosed early.
  • Eye research review (Expert Opin Biol Ther, 2018, PMID 30063853): describes Tβ4 eye drops being developed for corneal and dry-eye conditions. This is a researcher's narrative review, not trial results.
  • We found no controlled human trials of products sold as "TB-500."

Not sure what an RCT or meta-analysis is? See the glossary.

Common myths

  • Myth: TB-500 is a clinically tested drug. Clinical research has used thymosin beta-4 itself, not the online TB-500 product.
  • Myth: it speeds up any injury. Most repair data comes from lab and animal studies.
