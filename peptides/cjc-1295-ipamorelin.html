CJC-1295 & ipamorelin: the 'GH stack', evidence & dosage questions

Research purposes only Checked against official sources Oct 16, 2026

The straight answer

CJC-1295 and ipamorelin do raise growth-hormone signals in small studies, but there's no human trial of the stack, FDA has flagged safety signals, and neither is approved.

Quick facts

  • What they are: two lab-made peptides that push the body to release growth hormone.
  • Status: research purposes only. Not FDA-approved, on FDA's compounding safety-risk list, banned by WADA.
  • Human evidence: hormone-level studies for CJC-1295. A negative surgery trial for ipamorelin. None for the combo.
  • Dosage: no established human dose.
  • Bottom line: popular, thinly studied.

Dosing & timingEvidenceFAQ

What is CJC-1295 & ipamorelin?

CJC-1295 is a long-acting version of growth-hormone-releasing hormone. Ipamorelin mimics ghrelin, a hunger hormone that also triggers growth-hormone release. Online they're usually sold and talked about as a pair.

Is CJC-1295 & ipamorelin FDA-approved?

Neither is FDA-approved for any use. Both are on the FDA compounding safety-risk list: FDA flagged immunogenicity and impurity concerns, and says it has seen serious adverse events with CJC-1295, including increased heart rate and a whole-body flushing (vasodilatory) reaction. Unlike BPC-157, these were not among the peptides pulled off that list in April 2026. Both are banned at all times in sport under S2.2.4 of the 2026 WADA Prohibited List.

Rules vary by country and can change. We re-check and date-stamp this page.

CJC-1295 & ipamorelin dosage: what we actually know

There is no established or approved human dose for either, so we don't give one. What studies used:

  • CJC-1295 (PMID 16352683): single and repeated injections under the skin, dosed by body weight, in healthy adults. The authors said 30 or 60 µg/kg was relatively well tolerated. One study, short term, hormone levels only.
  • Ipamorelin (PMID 25331030): 0.03 mg/kg by IV twice daily for up to 7 days in hospital after surgery. Not comparable to home injections.

Why online numbers don't agree

  • Online "CJC-1295" may be the long-acting version from studies (with DAC) or a short-acting one without it. They behave very differently.
  • Study doses were per kilogram, by different routes.
  • Product content varies, and there is no trial of the combination to anchor anything.

Good questions to bring to a clinician

  • What is actually in the product, and has it been third-party tested?
  • Is there any human evidence for my specific goal, or only animal studies?
  • Could it interact with my other medicines or conditions?
  • Is there an FDA-approved option for what I'm trying to treat?
  • How would we know if it's working, or causing harm?

What the human evidence says

  • CJC-1295 (J Clin Endocrinol Metab, 2006): small placebo-controlled studies in healthy adults. One shot raised growth hormone 2–10× for 6+ days and IGF-1 1.5–3× for 9–11 days. These measured hormone levels, not muscle, fat or recovery.
  • Ipamorelin (Int J Colorectal Dis, 2014): a 114-patient trial after bowel surgery, given by IV in hospital. It was well tolerated but did not beat placebo on recovery of gut function.
  • The combo: we found no published human trial of CJC-1295 plus ipamorelin together.

Myths, busted

  • Myth: "it's natural, it just boosts your own GH." Raising GH and IGF-1 is the effect, and FDA has flagged real safety signals. Natural pathway isn't the same as safe.
  • Myth: "the stack is proven." There is no human trial of the combination.

CJC-1295 & ipamorelin FAQ

What is the CJC-1295 ipamorelin dosage?

There is no established human dose for either, and no human trial of the combination. Talk to a licensed clinician.

Is CJC-1295 FDA-approved?

No. FDA lists it among compounding substances that may present significant safety risks, citing serious adverse events like increased heart rate.

Does ipamorelin work?

Its one larger human trial, after bowel surgery, found it well tolerated but no better than placebo on its main goal.

Are CJC-1295 and ipamorelin banned in sport?

Yes. Both are named under S2.2.4 of the 2026 WADA Prohibited List, banned at all times.
