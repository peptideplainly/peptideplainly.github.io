What is MOTS-c?

MOTS-c is a short peptide your mitochondria make. Mitochondria are the energy factories in your cells. In mice, MOTS-c changes how the body handles sugar and fat (Cell Metab 2015), which is why fans call it "exercise in a vial."

Is MOTS-c FDA-approved?

Not FDA-approved. In July 2026 an FDA advisory committee voted to recommend allowing it in pharmacy compounding (FDA meeting page; vote report). The vote is non-binding. Banned in sport (WADA).

Not FDA-approved; sold for research only. Known risks: not well studied; there are no published human treatment trials yet. How to check a lab report.

Dosage

There's no established human dose, so we don't list one. A registered Phase 2a trial (NCT07505745) may give the first real human data.

What researchers have found so far

  • No published human treatment trials that we could find in PubMed as of October 2026. A Phase 2a trial in prediabetes is registered (NCT07505745) but has no results.
  • What human data exists is observational. One 2026 study found people with obesity had higher blood MOTS-c than lean people, and levels didn't change after bariatric surgery (J Clin Transl Endocrinol). That's measuring it, not giving it.
  • Most claims come from lab and mouse studies, for example its immune effects (eLife, 2026).

MOTS-c FAQ

What is the MOTS-c dosage?

There's no established human dose because it's research-only. Human trials are just starting.

Is MOTS-c FDA-approved?

No. An FDA advisory panel narrowly recommended it for a compounding list in July 2026, but that's non-binding and isn't drug approval.

Is MOTS-c banned for athletes?

Yes. The 2026 WADA Prohibited List names MOTS-c under S4 metabolic modulators.

Does MOTS-c work like exercise?

In mice it improved how the body handles sugar and fat (Cell Metab 2015), which is where the nickname comes from. Human trials will show how much carries over.
