PT-141 (bremelanotide, Vyleesi): label use, dosing & evidence

FDA-approved (as Vyleesi) Checked against official sources Oct 16, 2026

The straight answer

Bremelanotide is FDA-approved as Vyleesi for one group, premenopausal women with low sexual desire, and the label caps it at 8 doses a month.

Quick facts

  • What it is: a melanocortin-receptor drug that acts on desire in the brain.
  • Status: FDA-approved as Vyleesi for premenopausal women with HSDD. Not for men or postmenopausal women.
  • How it's taken: an as-needed autoinjector, at least 45 minutes before sex.
  • Evidence: two Phase 3 RCTs with 1,267 women. Modest effects.
  • Most common side effects: nausea, flushing, injection-site reactions, headache, vomiting.

Dosing & timingEvidenceFAQ

What is PT-141?

PT-141 is the old research code for bremelanotide. It acts on melanocortin receptors in the brain, which are involved in sexual desire. The approved product is Vyleesi, an autoinjector.

Is PT-141 FDA-approved?

FDA-approved (2019) as Vyleesi, only for premenopausal women with acquired, generalized low sexual desire (HSDD) that causes distress and isn't due to another condition, relationship problems, or a medicine. The label says it is not for postmenopausal women, men, or to enhance sexual performance (Vyleesi label). "PT-141" vials sold for research are not Vyleesi.

Rules vary by country and can change. We re-check and date-stamp this page.

Vyleesi dosing & timing: what the FDA label says

Your prescriber sets your dose. This is a summary of the official FDA label to help you follow along, not a plan to start, change, or stop anything.

Vyleesi (autoinjector) — FDA label on DailyMed

  • 1.75 mg injected under the skin of the abdomen or thigh, as needed, at least 45 minutes before expected sexual activity.
  • No more than one dose in 24 hours.
  • More than 8 doses a month is not recommended.

"PT-141" vials sold for research have no label. Their content isn't verified.

What the human evidence says

  • RECONNECT trials (Obstet Gynecol, 2019): two 24-week randomized trials, 1,267 premenopausal women. Desire and distress scores improved modestly but significantly vs placebo. Nausea, flushing and headache hit 10%+ of users.
  • Long-term extension (Obstet Gynecol, 2019): open-label follow-up of the same program.

Myths, busted

  • Myth: "it's Viagra for everyone." It's approved for one group: premenopausal women with HSDD. It works on desire in the brain, not blood flow.
  • Myth: "research PT-141 is the same." Only Vyleesi is made and checked to FDA standards.

PT-141 FAQ

Is PT-141 FDA-approved?

Bremelanotide, the drug called PT-141 in research, is FDA-approved as Vyleesi for premenopausal women with acquired, generalized HSDD.

When do you take Vyleesi?

The label says at least 45 minutes before expected sexual activity, as needed, no more than once in 24 hours.

How many times a month can you use Vyleesi?

The label says more than 8 doses per month is not recommended.

Is PT-141 approved for men?

No. The Vyleesi label says it isn't indicated for men or to enhance sexual performance.
