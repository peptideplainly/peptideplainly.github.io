What is BPC-157?

BPC-157 ("Body Protection Compound-157") is a 15-amino-acid peptide originally derived from a protein in stomach juice, and it's one of the most popular recovery peptides on the planet.

Is BPC-157 FDA-approved?

Not FDA-approved. In July 2026 an FDA advisory committee voted to recommend allowing it in pharmacy compounding (FDA meeting page; vote report). The vote is non-binding. Banned in sport (WADA).

Not FDA-approved; sold for research only. Known risks: not well studied; there's no clinical safety data in people yet; the review also flags risks from unregulated manufacturing and contamination (Vasireddi et al., HSS Journal 2025). Choose a tested source.

Dosage

There's no established human dose, so we don't list one. Most numbers online trace back to rat and mouse studies dosed by body weight.

What researchers have found so far

  • Systematic review, 2026 (Acta Orthop Belg, PMID 42885452): found 3 small human studies with 30 people in total: knee injections (16 people), bladder instillation (12) and an IV safety pilot (2). Most participants reported improvement, with no treatment-related side effects. Because the studies are small and early, with no control groups, the authors rated the certainty of evidence very low and call for proper clinical trials.
  • Systematic review, 2025 (Vasireddi et al., HSS Journal 2025, PMID 40756949, PMID 40756949): 36 studies (35 preclinical, 1 clinical; searched to June 2024) showing better healing of muscle, tendon, ligament and bone injuries in animal models. It rates the evidence level IV–V and found no clinical safety data in humans. Its one human study is the knee-injection chart review below.
  • Knee injection study, 2021 (Altern Ther Health Med, PMID 34324435): a look back at 16 patients who got BPC-157 knee injections. 14 of 16 (87.5%) reported knee pain relief, including 11 of the 12 who got BPC-157 alone. (The 2025 review above reports this study as 7 of 12 with relief lasting over 6 months.) It's a small, uncontrolled study.

BPC-157 FAQ

What is the BPC-157 dosage?

There's no established human dose because it's research-only. Studies have mostly been in animals, using a range of routes.

Is BPC-157 FDA-approved?

No. It's sold for research purposes only. WADA lists it as prohibited in sport.

Does BPC-157 work in humans?

It hasn't been tested in controlled human trials. A 2026 review found 3 small, uncontrolled studies with 30 people in total; most reported improvement, but the authors rated the certainty of evidence very low.

Is BPC-157 safe?

There's no clinical safety data in humans yet (Vasireddi et al., HSS Journal 2025). Animal studies reported no harmful effects, but unregulated products can be contaminated or mislabeled.

Why do BPC-157 doses online differ so much?

Most numbers come from animal studies, which used different amounts and routes.
