BPC-157: benefits, research & what people use it for

Why people are excited

The recovery peptide with a cult following.

  • 36 studies, mostly animal, report effects on tendon, muscle and gut healing.
  • In one small human study, 14 of 16 people with knee pain reported relief after BPC-157 knee injections.
  • Built from a protein found naturally in stomach juice.

Quick facts

  • What it is: a lab-made peptide of 15 amino acids, popular online for injury recovery.
  • Status: research purposes only. Not FDA-approved.
  • Research: promising early research. A large animal literature on tendon, muscle and gut healing, plus small early human reports.
  • Dosage: there is no established human dose. Online numbers mostly trace back to animal studies.
  • Why it's popular: the go-to recovery peptide, backed by a big body of animal research.

What is BPC-157?

BPC-157 ("Body Protection Compound-157") is a 15-amino-acid peptide originally derived from a protein in stomach juice, and it's one of the most popular recovery peptides on the planet.

Is BPC-157 FDA-approved?

Not an FDA-approved drug for any use. It is banned at all times in sport: the 2026 WADA Prohibited List names BPC-157 under S0 (non-approved substances). In 2023 the FDA listed BPC-157 among bulk substances that "may present significant safety risks" for compounding, noting limited human safety information. In April 2026 it was removed from that category after its nomination was withdrawn, which does not mean it was approved. Its compounding status is under FDA advisory-committee review (FDA page). It is widely sold online as a "research chemical."

BPC-157 dosage: what we actually know

There is no established or approved human dose for BPC-157, so we don't give one. Here's what the research shows instead:

  • Human reports: the 2026 systematic review (PMID 42885452) found only three: knee joint injections (16 people), bladder instillation (12 people), and an IV safety pilot (2 people). These are very different routes, and the abstract doesn't report doses.
  • Animal studies: most BPC-157 research is in rats and mice (PMID 40756949), dosed by body weight. That doesn't convert neatly to people.

Why the numbers online don't agree

  • Many online "protocols" are converted from animal studies, where doses are set by body weight in rats or mice. Those don't translate directly to people.
  • Where human studies exist, they've used different routes and settings, with no agreed standard.
  • Unregulated products can vary in what they actually contain.
  • Sellers and influencers often share numbers without sources.

What the research shows

  • Systematic review, 2026 (Acta Orthop Belg, PMID 42885452): found 3 small human studies with 30 people in total: knee injections (16 people), bladder instillation (12) and an IV safety pilot (2). Most participants reported improvement, with no treatment-related side effects. Because the studies are small and early, with no control groups, the authors rated the certainty of evidence very low and call for proper clinical trials.
  • Systematic review, 2025 (HSS Journal, PMID 40756949): 36 studies, almost all in animals, showing better healing of muscle, tendon, ligament and bone injuries. Its one human study is the knee-injection chart review below. The authors note there is no clinical safety data in humans yet.
  • Knee injection study, 2021 (Altern Ther Health Med, PMID 34324435): a look back at 16 patients who got BPC-157 knee injections. 14 of 16 (87.5%) reported knee pain relief, including 11 of the 12 who got BPC-157 alone. (The 2025 review above reports this study as 7 of 12 with relief lasting over 6 months.) It's a small, uncontrolled study.

BPC-157 FAQ

What is the BPC-157 dosage?

There's no established human dose because it's research-only. Studies have mostly been in animals, using a range of routes.

Is BPC-157 FDA-approved?

No. It's sold for research purposes only. WADA lists it as prohibited in sport.

Does BPC-157 work in humans?

Early results are encouraging. A 2026 review found 3 small human studies with 30 people in total, and most participants reported improvement with no treatment-related side effects. The studies are small and early, so bigger controlled trials are the next step.

Is BPC-157 safe?

Human safety data is still limited. If you buy for research, choose a source with third-party lab testing.

Why do BPC-157 doses online differ so much?

Most numbers come from animal studies, which used different amounts and routes.
