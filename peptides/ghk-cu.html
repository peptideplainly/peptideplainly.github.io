GHK-Cu (copper peptide), explained plainly

What it is

GHK is a tiny natural peptide made of three amino acids (glycine, histidine, lysine) that binds copper, which gives the name GHK-Cu. It's best known as a skincare ingredient.

Regulatory status

It's common in skincare products (often listed as "copper tripeptide-1"). U.S. cosmetics and their ingredients don't need FDA premarket approval. GHK-Cu isn't an FDA-approved drug. For injectable use, FDA has cited immunogenicity risk and limited human data (FDA page), and its compounding status is under review.

Regulatory status checked against official sources on October 16, 2026. Rules vary by country and change over time.

What the human evidence says

  • Review, 2018 (Int J Mol Sci, PMID 29986520): summarizes lab, animal, and gene-expression research suggesting roles in skin repair and collagen production. The evidence comes mainly from lab and gene-expression data, not large human clinical trials.
  • Most other recent research is in cells or animals, for example a rat ligament-healing model (J Orthop Res, 2015, PMID 25731775).
  • We did not find large randomized trials of GHK-Cu in people, injected or applied to the skin.

Not sure what an RCT or meta-analysis is? See the glossary.

Common myths

  • Myth: it reverses aging. Lab findings on gene activity don't show anti-aging effects in people.
  • Myth: skincare results apply to injections. Skin creams and injected research products are very different things.
