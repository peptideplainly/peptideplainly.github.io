GHK-Cu (copper peptide): skincare vs injectable, evidence & dosage questions

Research purposes only (injectable)

Quick answer

  • What it is: a tiny natural copper-binding peptide, common in skincare.
  • Status: a familiar cosmetic ingredient on the skin. Injectable use is for research purposes only and isn't FDA-approved.
  • Evidence: mostly lab and animal studies. We found no large human randomized trials.
  • Dosage: there is no established injectable human dose. For skincare, follow the product's directions.
  • Bottom line: promising lab biology, but limited proof in people.

Dosing & timing · Evidence · FAQ

What is GHK-Cu (copper peptide)?

GHK is a tiny natural peptide made of three amino acids (glycine, histidine, lysine) that binds copper, which gives the name GHK-Cu. It's best known as a skincare ingredient.

Is GHK-Cu FDA-approved?

It's common in skincare products (often listed as "copper tripeptide-1"). U.S. cosmetics and their ingredients don't need FDA premarket approval. GHK-Cu isn't an FDA-approved drug. For injectable use, FDA has cited immunogenicity risk and limited human data (FDA page), and its compounding status is under review.

Checked against official sources on October 16, 2026. Rules vary by country and can change.

GHK-Cu dosage: what we actually know

  • Skincare: GHK-Cu is used in topical cosmetics. Use those as directed on the label.
  • Injectable: there's no established human dose. FDA has noted limited human data and immunogenicity concerns for injectable use.
  • Much of the research is in cells and animals, for example a rat ligament model (PMID 25731775) and gene-expression work (PMID 29986520).

Why the numbers online don't agree

  • Many online "protocols" are converted from animal studies, where doses are set by body weight in rats or mice. Those don't translate directly to people.
  • The few human reports use different routes and settings, with no agreed standard.
  • Unregulated products can vary in what they actually contain.
  • Sellers and influencers often share numbers without sources.

What the human evidence says

  • Review, 2018 (Int J Mol Sci, PMID 29986520): summarizes lab, animal, and gene-expression research suggesting roles in skin repair and collagen production. The evidence comes mainly from lab and gene-expression data, not large human clinical trials.
  • Most other recent research is in cells or animals, for example a rat ligament-healing model (J Orthop Res, 2015, PMID 25731775).
  • We did not find large randomized trials of GHK-Cu in people, injected or applied to the skin.

Common myths

  • Myth: it reverses aging. Lab findings on gene activity don't show anti-aging effects in people.
  • Myth: skincare results apply to injections. Skin creams and injected research products are very different things.

GHK-Cu FAQ

Is GHK-Cu safe in skincare?

It's a widely used cosmetic ingredient. U.S. cosmetics don't need FDA premarket approval, so follow product directions and patch-test if you have sensitive skin.

What is the GHK-Cu injection dosage?

There's no established human dose for injectable GHK-Cu, and it isn't FDA-approved for that use.

Does GHK-Cu reverse aging?

Lab and gene-expression studies are intriguing, but we found no large human trials showing anti-aging effects.
