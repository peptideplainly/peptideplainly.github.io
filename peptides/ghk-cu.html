GHK-Cu: the copper peptide behind glowing-skin routines

What researchers are excited about: the copper peptide tied to skin repair and collagen (lab research)

What is GHK-Cu?

GHK is a tiny natural peptide made of three amino acids (glycine, histidine, lysine) that binds copper, which gives the name GHK-Cu. It's best known as a skincare ingredient.

Is GHK-Cu FDA-approved?

A staple skincare ingredient (often listed as "copper tripeptide-1"); cosmetics don't need FDA premarket approval. Injectable GHK-Cu isn't an FDA-approved drug.

Injectable GHK-Cu isn't FDA-approved; sold for research only. Known risks: for injectable use, possible immune reactions and limited human data (FDA). On the skin, use products as directed. Choose a tested source.

How it's used

Skincare: serums and creams, used as directed on the label. Injectable: there's no established human dose, so we don't list one.

What researchers have found so far

  • Review, 2018 (Int J Mol Sci, PMID 29986520): summarizes lab, animal, and gene-expression research suggesting roles in skin repair and collagen production. The evidence comes mainly from lab and gene-expression data, not large human clinical trials.
  • Most other recent research is in cells or animals, for example a rat ligament-healing model (J Orthop Res, 2015, PMID 25731775).
  • We did not find large randomized trials of GHK-Cu in people, injected or applied to the skin.

GHK-Cu FAQ

Is GHK-Cu safe in skincare?

It's a widely used cosmetic ingredient. U.S. cosmetics don't need FDA premarket approval, so follow product directions and patch-test if you have sensitive skin.

What is the GHK-Cu injection dosage?

There's no established human dose for injectable GHK-Cu, and it isn't FDA-approved for that use.

Does GHK-Cu reverse aging?

Lab and gene-expression studies link it to skin repair and collagen production. Large human anti-aging trials haven't been done yet.
