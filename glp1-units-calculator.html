The math, plainly

  1. Concentration (mg/mL) = total mg in the vial ÷ mL of liquid in it. If your label already says mg/mL, use that.
  2. mL to draw = your prescribed dose in mg ÷ concentration in mg/mL.
  3. Units on a U-100 syringe = mL × 100.

That's it. Three steps, and the concentration is the one that matters most: the same mg dose is a different number of units in a different-strength vial.

Why people mix these up

Milligrams (mg) measure the drug. Milliliters (mL) and syringe "units" measure liquid. The FDA has received reports of dosing errors with compounded semaglutide where people confused mg, mL and units, including patients told to draw 5 units (0.05 mL) on a U-100 syringe who drew 50 units instead. FDA says compounded concentrations can vary, even from one compounder, and that patients should talk with their prescriber or compounder about how to measure their dose. The FDA-approved pens (Ozempic, Wegovy) are dosed in mg and come prefilled.

Units calculator FAQ

How many units is 1 mL on an insulin syringe?

On a U-100 insulin syringe, 1 mL is 100 units, so 0.1 mL is 10 units and 0.01 mL is 1 unit. 'Units' on the syringe are a measure of volume, not of drug.

How do I convert mg to units?

Divide your prescribed dose in mg by your vial's concentration in mg per mL to get mL. Multiply mL by 100 to get units on a U-100 syringe. The concentration is the number that changes everything, so take it from your own vial label or pharmacy.

Why does the same dose come out to different units?

Because concentrations vary. FDA notes that compounded semaglutide can come in different concentrations, even from the same compounder, so the same mg dose can be a different number of units.

Does this calculator tell me what dose to take?

No. It only converts the dose your prescriber gave you. It never suggests a dose. Always follow your prescriber and pharmacist and double-check with them.
