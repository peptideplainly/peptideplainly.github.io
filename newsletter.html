Week of November 13, 2026 · The peptides that did more than shrink waistlines

Peptides are having a moment. And the best part isn't just the scale.

Big trials found real wins for the heart, the kidneys and sleep, too. Here's what they showed, in plain English.

Quick note: This is education, not medical advice. Every result links to the study, so you can check it yourself.

Heads up: we wrote each summary from the study's published abstract (the short summary at the top of a paper). We didn't read the full papers, so some details may be missing.

Meet the two big names

Two peptide drugs lead the pack: semaglutide and tirzepatide.

Both have huge human trials behind them. Let's look at what those trials found.

Win #1: Serious weight loss

In a trial called STEP 1, 1,961 adults took semaglutide. They had obesity or overweight, but no diabetes. They also made lifestyle changes.

After 68 weeks (about 16 months), they lost 14.9% of their body weight on average. The placebo group (people on a dummy shot) lost 2.4%.

Half of the semaglutide group lost 15% or more.

Tirzepatide's numbers were big too. In SURMOUNT-1 (2,539 adults without diabetes), people lost 15.0% to 20.9% of their weight in 72 weeks, depending on the dose. Placebo: 3.1%.

On the highest dose, 57% of people lost at least 20%. That's a fifth of their body weight.

Win #2: Fewer heart attacks and strokes

The SELECT trial followed 17,604 people for about 40 months. They were 45 or older, had heart disease, and had a BMI of 27 or higher. None had diabetes.

On semaglutide, 6.5% had a heart attack, a stroke or a heart-related death. On placebo, 8.0% did.

That's a 20% lower risk. In everyday terms, about 1.5 fewer people out of every 100.

Win #3: Backup for your kidneys

The FLOW trial tested semaglutide in 3,533 people with type 2 diabetes and long-term kidney disease.

Researchers tracked the big, scary events: kidney failure, losing half or more of kidney function, or dying from kidney or heart causes.

Semaglutide cut the risk of those events by 24%. People were followed for a typical 3.4 years.

The trial ended early, at a check-in that was planned from the start.

Kidney events on their own: 21% lower risk. Death from any cause: 20% lower.

Win #4: Better breathing at night

Sleep apnea means your breathing keeps stopping and starting while you sleep.

SURMOUNT-OSA was two trials in adults with obesity and moderate-to-severe sleep apnea. At the start, their breathing paused about 50 times an hour.

After 52 weeks on tirzepatide, that dropped by 25 to 29 pauses an hour. On placebo, it dropped by about 5.

The drop showed up whether or not people already used a CPAP machine (the mask that keeps your airway open at night).

The fine print (30 seconds, promise)

The most common side effects were stomach issues, like nausea and diarrhea. Most were mild to moderate.

They still mattered. In SELECT, 16.6% of people on semaglutide stopped because of side effects or other health problems. On placebo, 8.2% stopped.

One more thing: these results come from specific prescription doses, in specific groups of people, with doctors watching. They don't carry over to other peptides or to unregulated "research" products.
